# NAVCORE-SoOP — one board, and what bolts onto it

## There is one PCB

Earlier notes in this repository describe "Rev A", "Rev B" and occasionally a third
revision. That was bookkeeping that outgrew its usefulness, and it made the design sound
staged when it is not. To be unambiguous:

> **There is a single board.** It is the one in `NAVCORE-SoOP.kicad_pcb`, the one costed
> in `docs/BUYING.md`, and the one that is ready to order. Every capability below attaches
> to *that* board.

What used to be called **Rev B** is a **firmware configuration** of the same PCB — five
MatekH743 pins this airframe does not use, repurposed for the signals-of-opportunity front
end, each already reaching a pad. Switching to it is a reflash and a soldered-on tuner, not
a fabrication run. `docs/PINMAP.md` has the pin-by-pin detail.

The design is modular not because it is unfinished, but because the interfaces were
brought out to pads and connectors up front.

## What is still free to claim

The reason more can be added later is that interfaces were brought out and **not** spoken
for. As of 2026-09-04:

| interface | pads | status |
|---|---|---|
| **CAN** | `P61` 5 V, `P62` CANH, `P63` CANL, `P64` GND | **free — the best expansion path.** Addressable, multi-drop, latched |
| **`SERIAL6`** (UART4) | `P71`–`P74` | **free and unclaimed.** See the note below |
| **`SERIAL1`** (UART7) | `P41`–`P46` | the companion / telemetry port — also where FPV and the recording camera take VBAT and GND |
| **`SERIAL2`** (USART1) | `TP5` TX, `TP6` RX | earmarked for the 360 lidar; free until that is fitted |
| **I²C1** | `J3.4` / `J3.5` via the GPS connector | shared bus — both rangefinders live here |
| **`SERIAL4`** (USART3) | — | **genuinely unrouted.** `PD8`/`PD9` stop at the MCU with no pad |

### `SERIAL6` is expansion, not RF — settled 2026-09-04

Its nets are named `RF_5V` / `RF_TX` / `RF_RX` / `RF_GND`, and two documents used to claim
the port in two different ways. Neither claim survived contact with the module map:

- the **SoOP tuner is analogue** — I/Q, RSSI and PPS onto `TP9`/`TP10`/`TP11`/`P44`. It
  wants no UART. The `RF_*` names are a fossil of an earlier architecture in which the
  receiver was a smart serial module.
- the **rangefinder is the I²C variant**, and lands on `J3`.

The names are left as they are on purpose: they appear **nowhere on silkscreen**, so the
fossil is invisible on the physical board, and renaming nets in the artwork days before a
fabrication order buys nothing and risks a diff nobody reviews.

## How to add one

Every module follows the same sequence, and the order matters:

1. **Fit the hardware and power it.** Confirm the current draw against the rail budget in
   `docs/HARDWARE.md` before trusting it in flight.
2. **Verify it publishes.** `RNGFND1_DIST`, `PRX1_*`, `OPTICAL_FLOW` — see the value move
   in a ground station, by hand, before the EKF is allowed near it.
3. **Only then set the backend type**, and only after that the EKF source.

Every backend ships **disabled** (`PRX1_TYPE 0`, `RNGFND*_TYPE 0`). This is deliberate:
enabling a backend whose hardware is absent does not degrade gracefully, it refuses to arm.
That behaviour is pinned by the `proximity_absent` SITL scenario.

**Do not touch `EK3_SRC2` / `EK3_SRC3` until a flow source is fitted and verified in
`FLOWHOLD`.** They already reference `VELXY 5`, and the pilot's RC9 switch reaches them.

## Two constraints worth restating

**Proximity is never an EKF position source.** `EK3_SRC*_POSXY` accepts only
`0=None, 3=GPS, 4=Beacon, 5=OpticalFlow, 6=ExternalNav`. A lidar ring and a rangefinder
give obstacle avoidance and `POSZ` — they cannot hold horizontal position on their own.
This is the single most common misreading of this airframe's sensing.

**The 9 V rail is not what FPV needs — and there is no VBAT pad either.** `BUCK9_PH` is
unroutable and the block is DNP, but a 25 mW AIO camera+VTX runs happily off the **+5 V**
rail at `P41`/`P46`. 25 mW EIRP is the Ofcom IR 2030
licence-exempt airborne limit anyway, so this is the sensible size of transmitter to fit.

Be precise about where power comes from, because the repo got this wrong until
2026-09-04: **this board has no VBAT pad or test point.** VBAT reaches only `J2`, `D1`,
`R4`/`R18`/`R40`, the four bulk capacitors and the two bucks — verified against the
artwork. `PF1` is a *schematic power flag*, not a footprint, and an earlier comment in
`design.py` calling it "the only VBAT pad" was simply false. Anything genuinely needing
7–26 V takes it from the battery harness or the ESC's own pads, off-board.

The dead 9 V rail therefore costs this project no capability, which is why fixing it does
not justify a respin on its own.
