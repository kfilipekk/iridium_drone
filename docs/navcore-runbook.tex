\section{How to use this, and how to verify it}

This is one document in the order you actually do things: order the board, order the
aircraft parts, assemble, power it up, and fly it. It exists because the same information
used to be spread across four draft documents (ORDER, ASSEMBLY, BUILD and BUYING) that had
drifted apart --- in three cases far enough to send you shopping for parts with nowhere to
put them. On 2026-09-19 the first three were folded into this one and deleted;
\file{docs/BUYING.md} survives as the order sheet generated from \file{docs/PARTS.csv}, the
decision record.

\textbf{Verify before you spend. Read the verdict, not a summary:}

\begin{quote}
\cmd{python3 tools/preflight.py} \quad$\rightarrow$\quad must print \good{READY TO ORDER}
\end{quote}

The verdict is \emph{derived} from a manifest of gated prerequisites, so an unrun tool,
a non-zero exit, a failed check or an unclassified manifest entry is a blocking failure.
It cannot be typed by hand and it cannot shrink silently. What it proves is that the
\emph{design} is orderable. It cannot prove anything about money, stock, or JLCPCB's own
upload preview --- those are Sections~\ref{sec:upload} onward.

\subsection{The four commands that matter}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\cmd{python3 tools/preflight.py} & the gate; must say READY TO ORDER \\
\cmd{bash tools/make\_order\_bundle.sh} & regenerate \file{fab/} and zip the order \\

\cmd{bash tools/build\_firmware.sh} & build \file{arducopter.apj}, pinned to Copter-4.7.0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Freshness}

\file{fab/stock-snapshot.json} is re-fetched from \file{jlcpcb.com} and
\file{tools/check\_stock.py} blocks the gate once it is older than \textbf{7 days}. It was
last refreshed 2026-09-19 and resolves \textbf{56 of 56} non-DNP codes with cover for two
boards. Everything else in \file{fab/} is regenerated by
\file{tools/make\_order\_bundle.sh}, which refuses to build the bundle if the copper layer
count is not 6.

\subsection{Parts worth checking before you pay}

Stock is no longer quoted in prose. It lives in the dated snapshot
\file{fab/stock-snapshot.json}, age-gated at 7 days by \file{tools/check\_stock.py}.

Two substitutions are \textbf{not optional}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \flag{\file{Y1} must be \file{C2682774}} --- a \emph{passive crystal}, CL 20 pF. The
visually identical \file{C2901556} is an \textbf{active oscillator}: same SMD3225-4P land
pattern, but pin 4 is VDD, which this board grounds. Substituting it gives a board that
never starts.
\item \flag{\file{Y2} must be \file{C5563878}.} Its predecessor \file{C22381771} was down
to \textbf{1 unit} with no published restock date when \file{check\_stock.py} was written.
\end{itemize}

\label{sec:upload}

\item Copper layer count: \textbf{6}

\subsection{Paying}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Coupons are once-per-order} and cannot be combined. Take the SMT one.
\item \textbf{6-layer promotions recur}; worth waiting a few days if you are not in a hurry.
\item \textbf{UK import VAT is 20\%}, collected by the courier above GBP 135 with an
£8--12 handling fee. It is not a JLCPCB charge and is easy to forget when comparing
against a UK-stocked alternative.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The bench measurements that could have cost you a purchase}
\label{sec:benchbounds}

Sort the bench items by what a result could move, because only one kind can reach your
wallet. \cmd{python3 tools/check\_bench\_bounds.py} does this sort now and the gate walks its
verdict, so the answer is printed rather than argued. Nine items became six on 2026-09-20,
in five kinds:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CLOSED} --- the answer was already fixed by a \emph{document} or by the
artwork, so there is nothing left to measure that could change the board. Three items left
the list this way: the grommet, \file{BATT\_AMP\_PERVLT} and the \file{J2} pin order. They
are not deleted --- \file{check\_bench\_bounds.py} still prints each with its evidence, and a
reconciliation step refuses if this list and the manifest ever disagree.
\item \textbf{BOUND} --- the design-side corner is already under the limit and the
measurement \emph{confirms} it. \file{U8}: 87\,\degC{} against 125\,\degC{}. \file{U9}:
104\,\degC{} on good copper against 150\,\degC{}, on a board \file{thermal\_vias.py}
measures at 7443\,mm\textsuperscript{2} of plane. The grommet: the \emph{metal} M3 head clears
copper at every hole by $+$0.08\,mm, and the flange is always the wider of the two, so a wider
grommet adds only silicone bearing on mask.
\item \textbf{PARAM} --- the result is a \emph{firmware constant}: \file{FLOW\_ORIENT\_YAW}.
The board carries the pin and has no value to change.
\item \textbf{MODULE} --- the property belongs to a \emph{bought part} that plugs in: the
RF front end, which is the Nooelec SAWbird+ IR, and flow quality, which is the off-board
camera. A shortfall is placement, shielding or a different module.
\item \textbf{DATA} --- the T3b record asserts nothing about the board at all.
\end{itemize}

The consequence is the one that matters before you pay: \flag{no bench result can force a
different board}. Every item is still measured, but it confirms a bound instead of
discovering one, so the order does not wait for the session. The old text here warned that a
hot \file{U8} meant a respin; that risk was closed on 2026-09-20 by the part swap (Part~10),
which moved the worst corner to 87\,\degC{} and left it on the board.

Three are now closed outright rather than bought around. Each names the evidence:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{0.26\textwidth}P{0.62\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Item} & \textbf{What fixed the answer, so nothing is left to measure} \\
\midrule
the grommet flange & the flange and the M3 cap head are \emph{concentric}, so the flange is
always the wider of the two and the metal always the narrower. A wider grommet adds only
silicone bearing on mask; it cannot make the metal reach copper. Z was already swept
2.0--4.5\,mm by \file{check\_fit.py}, which requires every purchase to cover the range, so
the design is \emph{invariant} over the grommet \\
\file{BATT\_AMP\_PERVLT} & the ESC's own manual publishes Scale=400, i.e. 40\,mV/A, i.e.

\subsection{T0 --- the mechanical checklist, before any loom is cut}

\subsection{Stack height}

\subsection{The two screw warnings --- read both}
\label{sec:screws}

\begin{quote}
\cmd{python3 tools/fasteners.py}
\end{quote}

\begin{itemize}
\item \flag{Motor to arm, skid sandwiched.} Needs 13.5 mm, so order M3 $\times$ 14. But
rounding 13.5 $\rightarrow$ 14 drives \textbf{5.5 mm into a thread designed for 4.0 mm}. A
motor boss is often only about 4 mm deep, and \textbf{a bottomed-out screw feels tight
while clamping nothing} --- that is the failure that throws a motor in flight. Measure the
tapped depth before fitting sixteen screws. M3 $\times$ 12 plus a washer is the usual fix.
\item \flag{FC to ESC to frame, the 30.5 mm stack bolt.} Needs \textbf{27.3 mm}, so M3
$\times$ 30. Rounding 27.3 $\rightarrow$ 30 puts \textbf{6.7 mm} into the press nut. If the
tapped depth is shallower than that the screw bottoms out and clamps nothing.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{This is the whole reason the buy list carries an M3 assortment rather than one
length.} \file{free\_island} is not involved; the point is that both joints are decided by
a measurement you have not taken yet, so own every plausible length.

\subsection{Screw heads against copper --- 0.08 mm}

\textbf{No copper is under any screw head, and the margin is thin.} The closest is
\file{TP20} at \textbf{2.83 mm} against a \textbf{2.75 mm} cap-head radius ---
\flag{0.08 mm}, and that assumes \textbf{no washers}. A washer, or a head even 0.1 mm over
nominal diameter, puts metal on copper. If you add washers, re-measure before trusting
this line.

\subsection{Things that will bite you}

\begin{itemize}
\item The pack is EC5; everything else is XT60. Use the adapter --- do not re-terminate a

\item The frame offers 16 $\times$ 16 and 19 $\times$ 19 motor patterns. The motors are 19 $\times$ 19.
\item Measure both tapped depths before committing sixteen screws.
\item Nine pads are unlabelled, and one of them is 5 V.
\item The ESC cable is a gamble until you buzz it.
\item \file{U18} is not fitted: the 9 V rail is DNP by design.
\item Confirm 1 oz outer copper on the order; 0.5 oz halves every trace's rating.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\file{J2}, the ESC connector --- confirmed rather than assumed}

Wired \textbf{1 GND, 2 VBAT, 3 M1, 4 M2, 5 M3, 6 M4, 7 CUR, 8 TEL} (pins 9/10 are the
shell tabs). Betaflight's board page for the SpeedyBee F405 V4 gives the same FC-side
order, so it \textbf{matches} --- no cable rework needed.

The vendor table reads oddly because it lists the two \emph{ends} of the cable in opposite
order, since they face each other. Reverse the ESC column and every pair lines up:
\file{GND}$\leftrightarrow$\file{GND}, \file{BAT}$\leftrightarrow$\file{VBAT},
\file{M1}$\leftrightarrow$\file{S1}, \ldots, \file{CUR}$\leftrightarrow$\file{CURRENT}.

\textbf{Buzz it anyway.} One automated read of a wiki table is not the same as measuring
the cable you received. An 8-pin JST-SH is re-pinnable with a fine pick --- a mismatch
costs a cable rework, not a respin.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Pin 8 is inert.} The ESC end is \file{N/A} --- stock BLHeli\_S has no telemetry
output at all. \file{SERIAL5\_PROTOCOL 16} and the \file{UART8\_RX} wire are correct and
cost nothing, but they will receive nothing until a telemetry-capable ESC (BLHeli\_32,
AM32) is fitted. That is why the board ships \file{INS\_HNTCH\_MODE 1} (throttle) rather
than 3 (RPM). The upgrade path needs no wiring: flash \textbf{BlueJay} or \textbf{AM32}
onto the ESC over BLHeli passthrough (\file{SERVO\_BLH\_AUTO 1} already enables it), set
\file{SERVO\_BLH\_BDMASK 15} for bidirectional DShot on motors 1--4, then
\file{INS\_HNTCH\_MODE 3} --- the RPM-tracked notch is the single biggest flight-quality win
available on this aircraft.
\end{quote}

\section{Part 8 --- T2: MCU alive}

A blank STM32H743 already has a bootloader: the ROM DFU loader, which runs when
\file{BOOT0} is high through a reset. This board has the button for it (\file{SW1} pulls
\file{BOOT0} to 3.3 V; \file{SW2} is reset), so the first flash is over USB and needs no
debug probe. Earlier drafts said ``bootloader over SWD first, a board with no bootloader
will not enumerate'' --- that was wrong for this MCU. SWD is the \emph{fallback}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Supply on \file{VBAT} (Section~\ref{sec:T1}); USB does not power the board. Data-capable
USB-C cable --- charge-only leads enumerate nothing.
\item \textbf{Hold \file{BOOT} (\file{SW1}), tap \file{RESET} (\file{SW2}), release \file{BOOT}.}
\cmd{lsusb} shows \file{0483:df11 STM32 BOOTLOADER}. If it does not, check \file{Y1} first
(the passive crystal --- Section~\ref{sec:order-board}), then fall back to SWD below.
\item \textbf{Flash the ArduPilot bootloader over DFU:}
\cmd{dfu-util -a 0 -s 0x08000000:leave -D Tools/bootloaders/NAVCORE\_SoOP\_bl.bin}
(built by \file{tools/build\_firmware.sh} step 3). The board re-enumerates as the ArduPilot
bootloader with the board's own ID.
\item Flash \file{arducopter.apj} from \file{tools/build\_firmware.sh}, pinned to
Copter-4.7.0, from a ground station or \cmd{Tools/scripts/uploader.py}.
\item Connect a ground station; confirm the board ID and read \textbf{every} boot message
--- every fitted sensor should probe, and nothing unfitted should be claimed.
\item \flag{Copy \file{copter-deadreckon-home.lua} to the microSD at \file{APM/scripts/} by
hand.} The build script does not package it into the \file{.apj}. Without it,
\file{FS\_EKF\_ACTION 0} ships with no GPS-loss response at all.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{SWD, if DFU never appears.} An ST-Link V2 on \file{TP1} (SWDIO), \file{TP2} (SWCLK),
\file{TP20} (GND) and \file{TP21} (+3V3 as the target-voltage reference), with
\cmd{st-flash write Tools/bootloaders/NAVCORE\_SoOP\_bl.bin 0x08000000} or
STM32CubeProgrammer. It gets the same bootloader onto the same address; from step 4 on
nothing differs.

\textbf{Why U8's bracket read 70--139\degC{} until 2026-09-20, and what changed.} The load
it was modelled at, 0.95 A, was a hand-copied budget that still counted the deleted
PMW3901/VL53L1X, the DNP CAN transceiver and an eight-sensor ToF ring that the LD06
replaced --- 281 mA of parts not on the board --- while missing the tuner's 100 mA on
\file{U10}. \file{design.LOADS\_5V} now derives the rail from the 3.3 V load lists and the
fitted sensors: \textbf{0.74 A} continuous. The 139\degC{} was retired as a budget error,
and the part was \emph{then} changed --- two steps worth keeping apart. The LMR33630 in its
8-pin HSOIC-8 package was measured against the artwork on 2026-09-19 and its courtyard is
0.80 mm too wide for \file{U8}'s pocket in every orientation (\file{docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md}
keeps those numbers); the \emph{same silicon} in the 12-pin RNX VQFN has a 3.9 $\times$
2.9 mm courtyard, and it was swapped in place on 2026-09-20, taking the worst corner from
117\degC{} to \textbf{87\degC}.

\flag{The rule that keeps it there:} nothing that is \emph{payload} draws from this board's
buck. The SAWbird+, the recording camera, an FPV AIO and the LD06 run from the
\textbf{payload 5 V BEC} (stage B2, ~GBP 4); with the first two alone on the FC rail the
worst corner would be 154\degC. \file{check\_modules.py} fails any module that is neither in
\file{design.LOADS\_5V} nor marked BEC-powered, so a load cannot land on \file{U8}
unbudgeted.

\textbf{Half of that bracket is now measured rather than assumed.} Neither package has a
case to bolt a heatsink to, so every watt leaves into whatever copper is attached --- and
\file{tools/thermal\_vias.py} reads that copper off the board for \emph{both} regulators.
Measured 2026-09-20, after the swap: \file{U8.1} has \textbf{1 via within 1.7 mm holding
7600.3 mm$^2$ of GND plane}, \file{U9.2} has 2 vias onto the same plane, and \textbf{no
legal position exists for another via on either part} (the search runs to 1.7 mm and refuses
in-pad vias, which would wick solder). The RNX is a leadframe package with \emph{no}
separate exposed pad, so that plane is the whole thermal path --- which is what the
datasheet's $R\theta_{JB}$ of 23.3 \degC{}/W is describing.
\flag{It still does not license a number} --- copper moves the bound monotonically, and only
a thermocouple converts it into degrees.

\textbf{What to do:} power it stack-assembled, logging to the card, ten minutes at steady
state. Read \file{TEMP[0]}. If you own a probe, put it on \file{U8} too and on \file{U9}'s
case once --- \file{U19} is 17.2 mm from \file{U8} and cannot speak for it, and one case
reading establishes the offset that \file{U19} then carries forward alone.

\textbf{Stop and ask if \file{TEMP[0]} exceeds about 85\degC{} or \file{U8}'s case exceeds
about 100\degC.}

\section{Part 11 --- T3b: the RF chain, and the single open question}

This is the one place the project is honest about not knowing.

\textbf{The Doppler solve is written and validated.} \file{tools/soop\_solver.py} runs
\file{SOLVER OK}, recovering a known position from clean geometry at \textbf{160.1 m median
at 5 Hz}, and reports a singular geometry rather than inventing a number.

\textbf{What is not known is what the shipping antenna and receiver actually deliver.} The
shipped \file{DopplerErrorModel} sigma is \textbf{180 m}; the measured divergence boundary
was taken at \textbf{20 m}. The SITL scenarios print this warning beside their own results:

\begin{quote}
\emph{``The 120 m divergence boundary DOES NOT APPLY here: it was measured at sigma 20 m
and this run is at sigma 180 m. Re-measure the boundary at the current sigma before reading
a hold/runaway verdict off it.''}
\end{quote}

\flag{Rescaling it by arithmetic would invent a measurement.} Only a bench RF measurement
moves this: a reference receiver, the real antenna, real geometry. Until then, do not read
a position-hold verdict off those scenarios.

\section{Part 12 --- T3c: IMU temperature calibration}

Free, and it attacks the dominant error term: inertial drift is quadratic and dominated by
attitude error leaking gravity into the horizontal channel, and gyro and accelerometer bias
are strongly temperature-dependent in a stack with almost no airflow. A Pixhawk FMUv6X does
this in hardware with IMU heaters; this board (like the MatekH743 it is pin-identical to) has
none, so ArduPilot's software equivalent, \file{INS\_TCAL*}, is the answer. It is deliberately
\emph{not} in \file{defaults.parm}: it is a learning mode, not a flight setting.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Run a full six-axis accelerometer calibration first --- it sets the
\file{INS\_ACC*\_CALTEMP} and \file{INS\_GYR*\_CALTEMP} the fit is referenced to.
\item Cool the board below its minimum operating temperature: a domestic freezer, in a
sealed bag against condensation.
\item Set \file{INS\_TCAL1\_ENABLE 2} (\textbf{2 = start learning}, not 1) and
\file{INS\_TCAL1\_TMAX} to the target; the same for \file{INS\_TCAL2\_*} --- there are two IMUs.
\item Power it up in a warm room and \textbf{do not let it move until the run completes.}
Movement invalidates the fit silently.
\item Let it rise through at least 25\degC{} of range, freezer to desk, allowing ten minutes
per temperature point. Then verify the fit with the offline plotting tool: the corrected
traces should sit near zero across the whole range.
\end{enumerate}

Do this with the \textbf{stack assembled}, not the board in free air, because the assembly
\emph{is} the thermal environment.

\section{Part 15 --- Audit: what is still manual, soldered, or unmeasured}

This is the audit you asked for. It is split into the three kinds of risk, because each is
removed by a different action: \emph{manual work} by buying a jig or an alternative,
\emph{hand soldering} by a stencil or an assembly tier, and \emph{unmeasured values} only by
a bench session.

\textbf{Two structural constraints worth naming, because neither is removable now. Note
that the first reads like a defect and is not --- it is the opposite, and worth reading for
how it was settled:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \file{J3}'s GPS clearance \textbf{passes, because the requirement was re-derived
rather than the board changed.} The clear run before \file{R21} is \textbf{5.30 mm}. The
table's old figure of 6.0 mm was a JST-GH \emph{class} nominal, and it assumed a bend
allowance that nothing in that corridor needs: a pre-wired GH shell exits its wires at the
rear, already parallel to the board, and the only neighbour is a 0402 resistor 0.55 mm tall.
Shell half-width 2.6 mm plus 1.0 mm of handling margin is 3.6 mm, so 5.30 mm clears by
1.7 mm. \textbf{Relocating \file{R21} was mapped and rejected} --- \file{TP20}'s courtyard
and a via fence leave no position in the span that yields 6.0 mm, so there was never a fix
available there. The residual risk is a bulky moulded boot, which is why a spare JST-GH lead
is on the order list and T2 fits the plug \emph{before} the headers are soldered.
\item \flag{The board is 45.1 $\times$ 46.1 mm, below JLCPCB's 70 $\times$ 70 mm Standard
minimum.} Confirmed against their capability page: Standard single-PCB assembly runs
70 $\times$ 70 mm to 460 $\times$ 500 mm, while Economic runs 10 $\times$ 10 mm up. So
Standard needs the board panelled with breakaway rails --- an extra fabrication step and an
extra thing to confirm in their preview, not a blocker. Economic avoids the panel entirely
but charges the feeder fees anyway and drops an LGA onto your bench (Section 15.1), which is
why Standard plus panelisation is the recommendation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{15.4 The three worst, ranked}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \flag{The RF sigma (T3b).} It is the only item that can invalidate the project's
premise rather than a build step, and no amount of desk work substitutes for it. Everything
downstream of it --- position-hold claims, the SITL scenarios' hold/runaway results --- is
explicitly not readable until it is measured.
\item \flag{\file{U8}'s junction temperature.} 87\degC{} at the worst corner against a
125\degC{} recommended maximum --- 38\degC{} of margin, after the 2026-09-20 swap to the
LMR33630ARNXR. It is now a number to confirm rather than one to rescue, and the payload BEC
is what stops a later addition eating the margin.
\item \flag{The ESC cable and the tapped depths.} Cheap to check, catastrophic to skip. Both
are on this list only because they are properties of parts somebody else manufactured, and
both are silenced by owning the M3 assortment and a multimeter.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{15.5 Design-level inefficiencies, for a future revision}

These do not affect this order. They are recorded because a respin is the only place they
can be fixed, and one of them (\file{U8}) may force one anyway.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{USB cannot power the board.} VBUS runs the PHY only. Every bench session needs
a VBAT supply, which is why T1 exists at all.
\item \textbf{Nine pads arrive unlabelled} (Section~\ref{sec:T3d}) because a three-character
label at the 0.80 mm minimum height covers a 2.84 mm pitch pad and its neighbours. More
silkscreen real estate, or fewer test pads, would remove a bench session.
\item \textbf{14 DNP designators still have pads and copper.} Correct for reversibility, but
it is unused copper on two rails.
\item \textbf{\file{-nofpv} is byte-identical to the default pair.} A variant that ships the
same bytes as another is a place for someone to pick the wrong file.
\item \textbf{The Economic variant saves one part and not the feeder fees}, which makes it a
worse deal than it reads as (Section 15.1).
\item \textbf{In4 rail fragmentation} is an accepted warning on this board.
\item \textbf{The 1620 MHz SAW filter is not stocked at LCSC}, so the RF front end needs a
second supplier and a separate board.
\end{itemize}

\section{Part 17 --- What is not built yet}

The board and its firmware are green. The project's \emph{premise} is not, and a clean gate
says nothing about it, so it is stated here rather than left to the repository's own notes.

\textbf{The Iridium Doppler solver exists and is validated; the chain around it does not.}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{0.29\textwidth}P{0.61\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Piece} & \textbf{Status} \\
\midrule
The least-squares Doppler solve & \good{built and validated} --- \file{tools/soop\_solver.py}.
Gauss-Newton over position and clock with an analytic Jacobian; it recovers a known position
exactly from clean geometry, and reports \emph{singular} on degenerate geometry rather than
inventing a number \\
SGP4 and real TLE ephemeris & \good{built and validated} (2026-09-20) --- \file{tools/soop\_solver.py
--ephemeris} propagates the vendored Iridium NEXT catalogue with SGP4 and converts
TEME$\rightarrow$ECEF including the Earth's rotation. A known position is recovered from
\emph{real} orbits to $\sim$260\,m median at 5\,Hz over 60 bursts. SGP4 propagation error is
not measured by it \\
Burst detection and frequency estimation & \good{built and validated} (2026-09-20) ---
\file{tools/soop\_burst.py}, from I/Q: energy detection plus a periodogram carrier estimate
that beats the burst's $\sim$120\,Hz FFT bin, measured at p90 2.4\,Hz at C/N0
$\approx$62\,dB-Hz on synthetic I/Q. The bench re-check on recorded I/Q stays a bench item \\
The solve ported to C for the H743 & \good{built and validated} (2026-09-20) ---
\file{firmware/soop/}, a differential test against the Python reference (agreement
$1.6\times10^{-8}$\,m) and a cross-compile for Cortex-M7 \\
The flight firmware around it & \flag{not built} --- nothing yet samples the MAX2112, runs
SGP4 in C, or hands a fix to the EKF on the aircraft \\
The route into the EKF & \good{built} (2026-09-20) --- an in-process \file{AP\_GPS} backend
(\file{GPS\_TYPE\_SOOP}), so the fix never leaves the vehicle. Registered and compiled into
both firmwares, and gated; that the EKF actually \emph{fuses} it is not yet proven ---
\file{tools/soop\_sitl\_test.py} exists but is not passing \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The solver also established the receiver's requirement, which was written down nowhere before:
\textbf{about 5 Hz of burst frequency precision over 40--80 bursts}. An 8.28 ms burst has
roughly 120 Hz of raw FFT resolution, so the carrier must be estimated well inside a bin ---
that is the number T3b is really trying to meet.
