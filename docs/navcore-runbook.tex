\section{How to use this, and how to verify it}

This is one document in the order you actually do things: order the board, order the
aircraft parts, assemble, power it up, and fly it. It exists because the same information
used to be spread across four draft documents (ORDER, ASSEMBLY, BUILD and BUYING) that had
drifted apart --- in three cases far enough to send you shopping for parts with nowhere to
put them. On 2026-09-19 the first three were folded into this one and deleted;
\file{docs/BUYING.md} survives as the order sheet generated from \file{docs/PARTS.csv}, the
decision record.

\textbf{Verify before you spend. Read the verdict, not a summary:}

\begin{quote}
\cmd{python3 tools/preflight.py} \quad$\rightarrow$\quad must print \good{READY TO ORDER}
\end{quote}

The verdict is \emph{derived} from a manifest of gated prerequisites, so an unrun tool,
a non-zero exit, a failed check or an unclassified manifest entry is a blocking failure.
It cannot be typed by hand and it cannot shrink silently. What it proves is that the
\emph{design} is orderable. It cannot prove anything about money, stock, or JLCPCB's own
upload preview --- those are Sections~\ref{sec:upload} onward.

boards. Everything else in \file{fab/} is regenerated by
\file{tools/make\_order\_bundle.sh}, which refuses to build the bundle if the copper layer
count is not 6.

\subsection{Parts worth checking before you pay}

Stock is no longer quoted in prose. It lives in the dated snapshot
\file{fab/stock-snapshot.json}, age-gated at 7 days by \file{tools/check\_stock.py}.

Two substitutions are \textbf{not optional}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \flag{\file{Y1} must be \file{C2682774}} --- a \emph{passive crystal}, CL 20 pF. The
visually identical \file{C2901556} is an \textbf{active oscillator}: same SMD3225-4P land
pattern, but pin 4 is VDD, which this board grounds. Substituting it gives a board that
never starts.
\item \flag{\file{Y2} must be \file{C5563878}.} Its predecessor \file{C22381771} was down
to \textbf{1 unit} with no published restock date when \file{check\_stock.py} was written.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The DNP list --- derived, never typed}

\textbf{One designator ships unpopulated: \file{R15}}, the 120\,$\Omega$ CAN bus terminator.
A CAN bus is terminated at its two \emph{ends}; fit \file{R15} (0402, across \file{CANH}
and \file{CANL}) only if this board is the last node on the bus. Revision D fits everything
else, including the CAN transceiver \file{U11} (on its own \file{+3V3\_CAN} LDO, \file{U21},
fed from the payload rail) and the payload buck \file{U20}.

Do not type this list --- \file{make\_order\_bundle.sh} derives it from the BOM into
\file{HOW-TO-ORDER.txt}.

\subsection{Check these on screen at upload}
\label{sec:upload}

\begin{itemize}
\item Outline and board size: \textbf{46.5 $\times$ 47.2 mm}
\item Copper layer count: \textbf{6}
\item Drill count and the 0.20 mm minimum
\item \textbf{The placement preview: nothing should need turning.} Every rotation and centre
in the CPL is fitted to JLCPCB's own footprint for that LCSC part
(\file{tools/jlc\_orientation.py}, 160 of 163 agreeing with the rule-based rotation it
replaced). A part that still looks turned is a finding --- report it rather than rotating
it by hand
\item \textbf{1 oz outer copper}, explicitly
\item \textbf{Panel by JLCPCB.} Standard assembly requires a single PCB of at least
$70 \times 70$~mm and this board is $46.5 \times 47.2$~mm, so the panel and its breakaway
rails have to come from somewhere. Their own panelisation is the low-risk answer: you then
confirm the panel outline in the same preview as the CPL.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Paying}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Coupons are once-per-order} and cannot be combined. Take the SMT one.
\item \textbf{6-layer promotions recur}; worth waiting a few days if you are not in a hurry.
\item \textbf{UK import VAT is 20\%}, collected by the courier above GBP 135 with an
£8--12 handling fee. It is not a JLCPCB charge and is easy to forget when comparing
against a UK-stocked alternative.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The bench measurements that could have cost you a purchase}
\label{sec:benchbounds}

Sort the bench items by what a result could move, because only one kind can reach your
wallet. \cmd{python3 tools/check\_bench\_bounds.py} does this sort now and the gate walks its
verdict, so the answer is printed rather than argued. Nine items became six on 2026-09-20,
in five kinds:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CLOSED} --- the answer was already fixed by a \emph{document} or by the
artwork, so there is nothing left to measure that could change the board. Three items left
the list this way: the grommet, \file{BATT\_AMP\_PERVLT} and the \file{J2} pin order. They
are not deleted --- \file{check\_bench\_bounds.py} still prints each with its evidence, and a
reconciliation step refuses if this list and the manifest ever disagree.
\item \textbf{BOUND} --- the design-side corner is already under the limit and the
measurement \emph{confirms} it. \file{U8}: 87\,\degC{} against 125\,\degC{}. \file{U9}:
104\,\degC{} on good copper against 150\,\degC{}, on a board \file{thermal\_vias.py}
measures at 7443\,mm\textsuperscript{2} of plane. The grommet: the \emph{metal} M3 head clears
copper at every hole by $+$0.08\,mm, and the flange is always the wider of the two, so a wider
grommet adds only silicone bearing on mask.
\item \textbf{PARAM} --- the result is a \emph{firmware constant}: \file{FLOW\_ORIENT\_YAW}.
The board carries the pin and has no value to change.
\item \textbf{MODULE} --- the property belongs to a \emph{bought part} that plugs in: the
RF front end, which is the Tallysman HC610 active antenna, and flow quality, which is the off-board
camera. A shortfall is placement, shielding or a different module.
\item \textbf{DATA} --- the T3b record asserts nothing about the board at all.
\end{itemize}

The consequence is the one that matters before you pay: \flag{no bench result can force a
different board}. Every item is still measured, but it confirms a bound instead of
discovering one, so the order does not wait for the session. The old text here warned that a
hot \file{U8} meant a respin; that risk was closed on 2026-09-20 by the part swap (Part~10),
which moved the worst corner to 87\,\degC{} and left it on the board.

Three are now closed outright rather than bought around. Each names the evidence:

\subsection{The two screw warnings --- read both}
\label{sec:screws}

\begin{quote}
\cmd{python3 tools/fasteners.py}
\end{quote}

\begin{itemize}
\item \flag{Motor to arm, skid sandwiched.} Needs 13.5 mm, so order M3 $\times$ 14. But
rounding 13.5 $\rightarrow$ 14 drives \textbf{5.5 mm into a thread designed for 4.0 mm}. A
motor boss is often only about 4 mm deep, and \textbf{a bottomed-out screw feels tight
while clamping nothing} --- that is the failure that throws a motor in flight. Measure the
tapped depth before fitting sixteen screws. M3 $\times$ 12 plus a washer is the usual fix.
\item \flag{FC to ESC to frame, the 30.5 mm stack bolt.} Needs \textbf{31.0 mm}, so M3
$\times$ 35. Rounding 31.0 $\rightarrow$ 35 puts \textbf{8.0 mm} into the press nut. If the
tapped depth is shallower than that the screw bottoms out and clamps nothing; an M3 $\times$ 33
plus a washer is the fix.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{This is the whole reason the buy list carries an M3 assortment rather than one
length.} \file{free\_island} is not involved; the point is that both joints are decided by
a measurement you have not taken yet, so own every plausible length.

\subsection{Screw heads against copper}

\textbf{No copper is under any screw head.} The closest is \file{TP9} at \textbf{3.32 mm}
from a hole centre against a \textbf{2.75 mm} cap-head radius --- 0.57 mm --- and the washer
under each head is nylon, so even a 7 mm washer that reaches the pad puts no metal on
copper. If the board has moved since this was compiled, \cmd{python3 tools/dimensions.py
--md} prints the current figure.

\subsection{Sequence}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Inspect the boards} (Section 4.1).
\item \textbf{Print the assembly drawings and the pad map.} Four unlabelled pads ---
Section~\ref{sec:T3d}.
\item \textbf{Dry-fit the FC into the centre plate.} Confirm nothing blocks the west
edge's underside, where the microSD ejects.
\item \textbf{Buzz the GH 8P to SH 8P ESC lead}, all eight pins end to end, against the
\file{J2} pinout below, before anything is powered.
\item \textbf{Sit a skid under each arm}, holes aligned. Motor on top, wires inboard.
Thread all four screws by hand before tightening any; tighten diagonally.
\item \textbf{Solder the ESC's capacitor first.} The ESC comes with a low-ESR electrolytic
capacitor in the box. Solder it across the ESC's battery pads, observing the polarity
marking, with the legs as short as possible. It soaks up the spike when the battery is
plugged in and the ESC's own switching spikes. Without it the board still survives a 5S
plug-in (32.5 V peak in simulation against the regulators' 38 V limit), but the margin
shrinks to 5.5 V. If the box has none, buy a 35 V, 470--1000\,\textmu F low-ESR part.
\item \textbf{The stack, bottom to top: ESC, grommets, FC.} Fit the silicone grommets into
the FC's four mounting holes; ESC into the frame on its 30.5 mm pattern; FC on top with
its aft edge (\file{J3}, \file{J12}) toward the antenna tower and the silkscreen arrow
pointing at the nose; \file{AHRS\_ORIENTATION} stays 0 (Section~\ref{sec:T0}).
\item \textbf{4 $\times$ M3 $\times$ 35 through the stack}, with the four 15 mm
female-female spacers setting the ESC-to-FC separation.
\item \textbf{The top plate on the bought standoffs} --- 25 mm at the front, over the
stack, 33 mm at the rear, in place of the kit's 22 and 30. Both sets change together or the
top plate is not flat.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Serial leads cross over.} The board's TX goes to the module's RX and the board's RX
to the module's TX: on \file{J5}, pin 2 (TX) to the receiver's RX and pin 3

\textbf{\file{J21}, the companion port --- three rules.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pin 1 is +5 V for a radio-class load only} --- a SiK radio, an ESP32 MAVLink
bridge. A companion computer (a Radxa wants 5 V at 2 A) takes its own BEC and uses pins 2--6,
with its ground joined to pin 6.
\item \textbf{It is a Pixhawk TELEM port}, so a lead made for one mates; check that the far
end crosses TX and RX. CTS and RTS are on pins 4 and 5; set \file{BRD\_SER1\_RTSCTS} to match

\item \textbf{It is the only spare serial port.} \file{SERIAL6} is unassigned (UART4's pins
became the PWM5 and PWM7 outputs), and \file{J11} is earmarked for the lidar. A second serial
device means giving one up.
\end{enumerate}

\flag{\file{J12} carries +5 V.} The antenna feed (\file{U24}, current-limited to 95--155 mA;
a dead short folds back to about 35 mA) is on from power-up, because \file{PC5} is pulled up
and the firmware keeps it on. That powers the HC610's LNA up the coax --- and it will push DC
into anything else you connect. \textbf{A bench SDR, a signal generator or a spectrum
analyser on \file{J12} or its pigtail goes through the DC block}, or put \file{ant\_feed 0}
in \file{APM/SOOP/soop.cfg} and reboot first. Never short the centre pin to test it.

\textbf{The ESC lead is GH 8P at the board and SH 8P at the ESC.} \file{J2} is wired in the
SpeedyBee's own order, so the lead is \emph{straight-through}: pin 1 to pin 1, through pin 8.

splice, all eight pins, before any VBAT}: pin 2 is the battery, and a lead with two pins
swapped puts VBAT on a motor signal or ground.

\textbf{Servos on \file{J17} and \file{J23}.} Each is a male 1$\times$3 header in the
standard order, so a servo's own female plug goes straight on: signal (orange, yellow or
white) on pin 1, red in the middle, brown or black on pin 3 --- check pin 1 on the
assembly drawing before pushing the plug home, because a reversed plug fits. The pins
overhang the fore edge; \textbf{fit the printed clip over both plugs} so a pulled lead loads
the clip and not the solder joints. For two 9\,g micro servos the header's own pin 2 feeds
them from \file{+5V\_PAYLOAD}. Anything bigger goes on its own 5--6\,V BEC, with the BEC's
ground joined to pin 3 and pin 2 left unconnected. \file{lander.parm} already drives
\file{SERVO7}/\file{SERVO8}; check the travel direction with the props off.

\textbf{Mount the compass as far from the ESC and the battery leads as the frame allows.}
There is no magnetometer on the FC by design: one 15 mm from a 60 A 4-in-1 is useless.

payload, not all of it}: every payload connector at once is over what \file{U20} should

One cable: the MMCX to SMA pigtail (15 cm, RG178 or RG316, straight MMCX, right-angle SMA).

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fit the HC610 to the tower seat} with its two M2.5 $\times$ 8 screws into its
own threads (6 mm deep --- never longer than 10), its SMA male pointing down the column.
\item \textbf{Mate the pigtail's SMA female onto the HC610's SMA} inside the column, finger

\item \textbf{Tie the coax round the column} just below the window, so the snap and the SMA

\item \textbf{Route it forward outboard of the battery} and in past the top plate's starboard

MMCX plug onto \file{J12} until it clicks. Tie it down 10--15 mm from the plug with a little
slack, so vibration works on the tie and not the snap.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{First power with the antenna on.} Check these at T2, once the firmware is on:
\begin{itemize}
\item The GCS shows \emph{SoOP: antenna ok, 15.0 mA} --- about 15 mA is the HC610's LNA.
\emph{SoOP: antenna open} (under 3 mA) means the pigtail is not mated or the antenna is
dead; \emph{SoOP: antenna short} (28 mA or more, or the switch's fault flag) means a short
on the coax --- the firmware turns a short that lasts 5 s off for 10 s and retries. The log
records it once a second as \file{SOA}.
\item The tuner locks: \emph{SoOP: tuner locked, VCO n} at boot (Part~11, step 2).
\item The orbits on the card are fresh: \file{APM/SOOP/iridium.tle} fetched within the
last few days. The filter ignores elements older than 7 days (Part~11, TLEs and time).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Things that will bite you}

\begin{itemize}
\item The pack is EC5; everything else is XT60. Use the adapter --- do not re-terminate a
hard-case pack on build day.
\item The frame offers 16 $\times$ 16 and 19 $\times$ 19 motor patterns. The motors are 19 $\times$ 19.
\item Measure both tapped depths before committing sixteen screws.
\item The ESC's own leads do not fit \file{J2}; the GH 8P to SH 8P lead is a gamble until you buzz it.
\item \file{J12} carries +5 V: a bench instrument goes on through the DC block.
\item A servo plug fits either way round on its header; signal is pin 1.
\item Four pads are unlabelled; identify every pad on the padmap, never by counting from a neighbour.
\item The board is \textbf{5S maximum} --- the input TVS sets it (Part~1).
\item Wire one mission's payload onto \file{+5V\_PAYLOAD}, not every payload at once.
\item Confirm 1 oz outer copper on the order; 0.5 oz halves every trace's rating.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\file{J2}, the ESC connector --- confirmed rather than assumed}

Wired \textbf{1 GND, 2 VBAT, 3 M1, 4 M2, 5 M3, 6 M4, 7 CUR, 8 TEL} on a JST-GH 8P
side-entry socket. Betaflight's board page for the SpeedyBee F405 V4 gives the same FC-side
order on the SpeedyBee's JST-SH 8P, so the GH 8P to SH 8P lead is straight-through --- no
pin is moved.

The vendor table reads oddly because it lists the two \emph{ends} of the cable in opposite
order, since they face each other. Reverse the ESC column and every pair lines up:
\file{GND}$\leftrightarrow$\file{GND}, \file{BAT}$\leftrightarrow$\file{VBAT},
\file{M1}$\leftrightarrow$\file{S1}, \ldots, \file{CUR}$\leftrightarrow$\file{CURRENT}.

\textbf{Buzz it anyway.} One automated read of a wiki table is not the same as measuring
the lead you have. A spliced lead is the easiest place to swap two wires.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Pin 8 is inert.} The ESC end is \file{N/A} --- stock BLHeli\_S has no telemetry
output at all. \file{SERIAL5\_PROTOCOL 16} and the \file{ESC\_TEL} wire are correct and
cost nothing, but they will receive nothing until a telemetry-capable ESC (BLHeli\_32,
AM32) is fitted. That is why the board ships \file{INS\_HNTCH\_MODE 1} (throttle) rather
than 3 (RPM). The upgrade path needs no wiring: flash \textbf{BlueJay} or \textbf{AM32}
onto the ESC over BLHeli passthrough (\file{SERVO\_BLH\_AUTO 1} already enables it), set
\file{SERVO\_BLH\_BDMASK 15} for bidirectional DShot on motors 1--4, then
\file{INS\_HNTCH\_MODE 3} --- the RPM-tracked notch is the single biggest flight-quality win
available on this aircraft.
\end{quote}

\textbf{If a rail is dead with a correct supply, look at \file{R46} before the buck.} It is

than a series diode. Use the XT60 keying and check twice.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{USB powers the logic, not the whole board.} \file{D\_USB} feeds VBUS into the +5\,V
rail at about 4.7\,V, which runs both 3.3\,V rails, the MCU and the sensors --- enough to
configure and flash it. The payload rail stays off (its buck needs about 6.6\,V), and

battery pin, to about 4\,V. Do the first power-up (this Part) on \file{VBAT} regardless.
\end{quote}

\section{Part 8 --- T2: MCU alive}

A blank STM32H743 already has a bootloader: the ROM DFU loader, which runs when
\file{BOOT0} is high through a reset. This board has the button for it (\file{SW1} pulls
\file{BOOT0} to 3.3 V; \file{SW2} is reset), so the first flash is over USB and needs no
debug probe. Earlier drafts said ``bootloader over SWD first, a board with no bootloader
will not enumerate'' --- that was wrong for this MCU. SWD is the \emph{fallback}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Supply on \file{VBAT} (Section~\ref{sec:T1}), as the board will be in flight; USB alone
would also run the MCU. Data-capable USB-C cable --- charge-only leads enumerate nothing.
\item \textbf{Hold \file{BOOT} (\file{SW1}), tap \file{RESET} (\file{SW2}), release \file{BOOT}.}
\cmd{lsusb} shows \file{0483:df11 STM32 BOOTLOADER}. If it does not, check \file{Y1} first
(the passive crystal --- Section~\ref{sec:order-board}), then fall back to SWD below.
\item \textbf{Flash the ArduPilot bootloader over DFU:}
\cmd{dfu-util -a 0 -s 0x08000000:leave -D Tools/bootloaders/NAVCORE\_SoOP\_bl.bin}
(built by \file{tools/build\_firmware.sh} step 3). The board re-enumerates as the ArduPilot
bootloader with the board's own ID.
\item Flash \file{arducopter.apj} from \file{tools/build\_firmware.sh}, pinned to
Copter-4.7.0, from a ground station or \cmd{Tools/scripts/uploader.py}.
\item Connect a ground station; confirm the board ID and read \textbf{every} boot message
--- every fitted sensor should probe, and nothing unfitted should be claimed.
\item \flag{Copy \file{copter-deadreckon-home.lua} to the microSD at \file{APM/scripts/} by
hand.} The build script does not package it into the \file{.apj}. Without it,
\file{FS\_EKF\_ACTION 0} ships with no GPS-loss response at all.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{SWD, if DFU never appears.} \file{J19} is a Tag-Connect TC2030-NL pad set (1 +3V3 as
the target-voltage reference, 2 SWDIO, 3 NRST, 4 SWCLK, 5--6 GND), reached with a
TC2030-IDC-NL cable and an adapter to the ST-Link's header --- neither is on the buy list,
because DFU is the route. Flash with
\cmd{st-flash write Tools/bootloaders/NAVCORE\_SoOP\_bl.bin 0x08000000} or
STM32CubeProgrammer. It gets the same bootloader onto the same address; from step 4 on
nothing differs.

\subsection{Antenna feed monitor bench test}
\label{sec:ant_bench}
The active antenna bias feed (\file{U24}, \file{U25}) supplies +5\,V on \file{J12} MMCX to power the HC610's internal LNA. Current is sensed by \file{U25} (INA180A2, $\times 50$) across \file{R62} (2.2\,$\Omega$) and digitized by ADC3 on \file{PC2\_C}. Verify all three operational states on the bench:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Open circuit (no antenna connected):} Probe \file{U25.1} (\file{ANT\_CURRENT}) with a DMM: output voltage sits near \textbf{0\,V} ($<$25\,mV, corresponding to $<$0.25\,mA). The GCS displays \cmd{SoOP: antenna open (0 mA)} once on transition, and the DataFlash \file{SOA} log records state \file{St=1} (\cmd{OPEN}) with \file{mA} under 3.0.
\item \textbf{Normal active load (HC610 antenna connected):} Plug the HC610 helical antenna into \file{J12} via the RG178 pigtail. The LNA draws nominal 15\,mA. \file{U25.1} reads approximately \textbf{1.65\,V} ($15\,\mathrm{mA} \times 2.2\,\Omega \times 50$). The GCS reports \cmd{SoOP: antenna ok (15 mA)}, and the \file{SOA} log records \file{St=2} (\cmd{OK}) with \file{En=1}.
\item \textbf{Short-circuit detection and automatic retry:} Do \emph{not} dead-short the coax centre pin directly to shield with a screwdriver. Instead, connect a \textbf{100\,$\Omega$ or 50\,$\Omega$ 1/4\,W test resistor} across an SMA pigtail on \file{J12} (drawing 50--100\,mA, well above the 28\,mA threshold). \file{U25.1} saturates near \textbf{3.25--3.30\,V}. After the 0.5\,s settle filter, the GCS reports \cmd{SoOP: antenna shorted (retry in 5s)}, and \file{SOA} records \file{St=3} (\cmd{SHORT}). After 5\,s of continuous fault, the firmware de-asserts \file{PC5} (\cmd{En=0}), turning \file{U24} off for 10\,s (\file{retry\_off\_s}) to protect the switch from dissipation. Disconnecting the test resistor allows the feed to recover to \cmd{OK} or \cmd{OPEN} on the next retry.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Everything optional ships disabled in \file{defaults.parm} and must be turned on
here.} That is deliberate: a rangefinder configured but absent fails prearm, and a bare
board arms out of the box. Do not enable a sensor you have not fitted.

\section{Part 11 --- T3b: the RF chain, and the single open question}

This is the one place the project is honest about not knowing.

\textbf{What exists.} The whole Iridium chain is written in C and gated against simulation,
and the aircraft runs the same C (\file{AP\_SoOP}, installed by
\file{tools/install\_soop\_backend.py}). The burst DSP finds 95\% of bursts at 55\,dB-Hz and
above with a p90 carrier error near 2\,Hz and no false detections
(\file{tools/check\_soop\_dsp.py}). The navigation holds a median of about 200\,m and a p95
of about 710\,m horizontally through 15--30 minute GNSS-denied flights at 15\,m/s on the real
constellation, with orbits one to three days old --- and the accuracy it reports holds about
97\% of the time, which is what the flight EKF needs (\file{tools/check\_soop\_nav.py}).
What limits it is the flight EKF's velocity once GNSS is gone, not the orbits: at 1\,m/s of
velocity error the median is 200\,m; at 0.1\,m/s, 36\,m.

\textbf{What is not known is what the real sky and this antenna deliver}: how many bursts
per minute, at what C/N0, how far each satellite's transmitter is off frequency, and how
the TCXO drifts in flight. The simulation's assumption for each is written in
\file{tools/soop\_scenario.py}. T3b replaces them with measurements, in three steps, and the
filter's settings (\file{soop\_nav\_default\_cfg} in \file{firmware/soop/soop\_nav.c}, or
\file{APM/SOOP/soop.cfg} on the card) are where the answers go.

\subsection{Step 1 --- record the sky with the bench kit (no board needed)}

Kit A2: the RTL-SDR, the HC610 antenna outdoors with open sky, and the Pi. The antenna's
LNA takes its 15 mA from the RTL-SDR V4's bias tee (\cmd{rtl\_biast -b 1}), so the bench
chain is the antenna and one cable.

\begin{verbatim}
bash sitl/tle/fetch_iridium_tle.sh                        # today's orbits
date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%S.%NZ > rec.time
rtl_sdr -f 1626333333 -s 2000000 -g 40 -n 1200000000 rec.cu8    # 10 min, 2.4 GB
python3 tools/soop_bench.py rec.cu8 --start $(cat rec.time) --site LAT LON H
\end{verbatim}

\file{--site} is where the antenna was, in degrees and ellipsoidal metres (a phone's GPS is
close enough). The tool converts the recording to what the board's ADCs would capture
(\file{firmware/soop/iq\_import.c}), runs the flight DSP and the flight navigation over it,
and reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bursts per minute and their C/N0.} The simulation assumes about one burst a
second from each satellite in view, at 45--65\,dB-Hz. A few per minute is a gain or antenna
problem, not a navigation one.
\item \textbf{The anchored run}, where the filter is told the site. Most bursts should be
fused and the \textbf{NIS} should sit near 1: that says the filter's noise model fits the
real sky. Well above 1, the first suspect is the satellites' transmit offsets, printed
beside it: put their rms in \file{soop.cfg} as \file{beta\_sigma}.
\item \textbf{The offset run}, started 5\,km away. It shows the filter walking in to the
site. Its reported accuracy is optimistic --- this is a blind start, which the aircraft
never uses (it anchors on GPS first).
\item \textbf{gr-iridium's count}, if it is installed: the DSP should find most of the
bursts gr-iridium finds in the Ring Alert band.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Step 1b --- self-interference, with the bench kit on the aircraft}

\file{tools/check\_rf.py} (\file{fly.rfbench}) gates ready-to-fly on four measurements in
\file{fab/rf-bench-results.json}. Start the file with
\cmd{python3 tools/check\_rf.py --template > fab/rf-bench-results.json}. Fit the HC610 in its
flight position on the tower, run its pigtail to the RTL-SDR on the ground beside the aircraft,
and take the four steps in one session, in this order, without moving the antenna:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{baseline}: aircraft unpowered;
\item \textbf{board\_powered}: battery in, motors off;
\item \textbf{motors\_spinning}: props off, tethered, about hover throttle;
\item \textbf{elrs\_tx}: as above, with the ELRS link transmitting.
\end{enumerate}

For each step, record five minutes with Step~1's \cmd{rtl\_sdr} line (\cmd{-n 600000000}),
then run \file{soop\_bench.py}. Its \emph{DSP: N bursts, n/min} line gives
\file{bursts\_per\_min}. For \file{noise\_floor\_dbm}, run
\cmd{rtl\_power -f 1616M:1626.5M:10k -g 40 -i 10 -e 60 floor.csv} and take the median bin. It is relative dB, not calibrated dBm, but the gain is the same in all four steps, so the steps can be compared.
Fill in \file{utc} and \file{sky} (clear, or the cloud cover), then run \cmd{python3 tools/check\_rf.py}.
A step passes if it keeps at least half the baseline's bursts. Because the steps are cumulative,
the first step to fail names the culprit: the board's switchers, then the ESC and motor leads,
then the control link.

\subsection{Step 2 --- the board on the bench, after T3}

On the SD card: \file{APM/SOOP/iridium.tle}, a copy of \file{sitl/tle/iridium-next.tle}
fetched that day, and optionally \file{APM/SOOP/soop.cfg} (\file{key value} lines:
\file{beta\_sigma}, \file{clkd\_q}, \file{bbg}, \file{swap\_iq}, \file{replay},
\file{dut1}, \dots). Set \file{GPS2\_TYPE 27}; the task starts with the SoOP GPS backend.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Tuner lock.} At boot the GCS shows \emph{SoOP: tuner locked, VCO n}, or an
error naming the VCO and the VTUNE ADC state (row 3.12). The LO is set from the 25\,MHz TCXO
in 23.8\,Hz steps, and the filter is told the one actually programmed.
\item \textbf{CPU, before any RF.} Copy \file{board.iq} from step 1's output folder to
\file{APM/SOOP/replay.iq}, add \file{replay 1} to \file{soop.cfg} and reboot. The log's
\file{SOS.CPU} is the DSP's share of real time on the H743; keep it well under 50\%. Remove
\file{replay} afterwards.
\item \textbf{Antenna feed.} The GCS shows \emph{SoOP: antenna ok, 15.0 mA} with the HC610
on \file{J12}; \emph{open} or \emph{short} is the coax or the antenna, not the sky
(Section~\ref{sec:plugs}). \file{SOA} in the log has it once a second.
\item \textbf{Live capture}, outdoors, the HC610 on \file{J12} through its pigtail; the board
feeds its LNA.
\file{SOS.Bps} (bursts per second) should be comparable to step 1. If the board hears nothing
the bench kit hears, try \file{swap\_iq 1} (swapped I and Q mirror the spectrum); if
\file{SOS.Ovr} counts up, the DSP is not keeping up with the 500\,kSPS it is fed. The
ADCs run at 1\,MSPS, the most \file{PA4}'s slow channel allows, averaged in pairs.
\item \textbf{Static with GPS, 30 minutes at a known spot.} \file{SOF.HAcc} settles to tens of
metres (anchored on GPS) and \file{SOF.NIS} near 1. \file{SOS.Clk} is the TCXO: how fast it
drifts over those 30 minutes sets \file{clkd\_q} (the default, 0.08\,Hz/s/$\sqrt{\rm s}$,
assumes the board temperature wanders by about 3\,\degC).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Step 3 --- in flight}

With \file{GPS2\_TYPE 27} the Iridium fix is the second GPS and \file{GPS\_AUTO\_SWITCH 1}
moves to it when the real one loses its fix. When denied, the EKF must take position from GPS
(\file{EK3\_SRC2\_POSXY 3}) and velocity from flow (\file{EK3\_SRC2\_VELXY 5}) --- not from
the Iridium fix, whose velocity is the EKF's own, corrected. Fly first with GPS healthy and
compare \file{SOF} against \file{GPS} in the log; then a denial test in a safe area.

\textbf{What the aircraft will not do.} It offers no Iridium fix until it has had GPS (or a
surveyed start within 50\,m): started blind, the filter linearises about a guess kilometres
out and in simulation reported up to four times better than it was.

\textbf{Spoofing.} From arming, a second copy of the navigation runs without the GPS, on
Iridium alone (\file{soop\_guard.c}), and every GPS fix is checked against it before the
navigation learns from it. A fix that jumps is refused at once; a GPS that stays further
from the Iridium-only position than 3 times its accuracy (and at least 150\,m) for 5\,s is
declared spoofed. The GCS then shows \emph{SoOP: GPS spoofed, N m from Iridium - using
SoOP}, the navigation drops the real GPS for the rest of the flight, and the autopilot is
moved onto the Iridium receiver (\file{GPS\_AUTO\_SWITCH 0}, \file{GPS\_PRIMARY 1}, not
saved: a reboot restores them). \file{SOS.G} logs the guard's state and \file{SOS.GD} the
distance it measured.

In simulation (\file{tools/check\_soop\_nav.py}) it raises no alarm on honest GPS ---
including GPS coming back after a 15 minute denial --- and catches a 500\,m jump in 1--2\,s,
a 10\,m/s drag in about 4 minutes, a 5\,m/s drag in about 4 and a 2\,m/s drag in 5 to 19;
by then the spoofer has moved the GPS 1.3--2.4\,km. It cannot be faster: a spoofer that
drags the position drags the EKF's velocity too, and until the aircraft has been pulled
further than an Iridium-only fix can tell apart, the two stories agree. A slower drag may
not be caught within a flight. Watch \file{SOS.GD} in flight: a distance that keeps growing
while the GPS looks healthy is the early sign.

\subsection{TLEs and time}

Before each flying day run \cmd{bash sitl/tle/fetch\_iridium\_tle.sh} and copy the result to
\file{APM/SOOP/iridium.tle}. It fetches CelesTrak's supplemental sets for Iridium, fitted to
Iridium's own ephemeris and marked by a \file{C} in column 8 of line 1; if those are
unavailable it says so and writes the public TLEs instead, which are several hundred metres
further off along-track. The filter weighs each satellite by which kind its elements are and
their age, and ignores any older than 7 days. If IERS Bulletin A gives $|$UT1$-$UTC$|$ above 0.1\,s, put it
in \file{soop.cfg} as \file{dut1}: 0.1\,s turns the satellites 46\,m.

The SITL scenarios (\file{sitl/}) still fly the synthetic \file{DopplerErrorModel} of
\file{sitl/soop\_link.py}, not this chain, so do not read a position-hold verdict off them
either.

\section{Part 12 --- T3c: IMU temperature calibration}

Free, and it attacks the dominant error term: inertial drift is quadratic and dominated by
attitude error leaking gravity into the horizontal channel, and gyro and accelerometer bias
are strongly temperature-dependent in a stack with almost no airflow. A Pixhawk FMUv6X holds
its IMUs warm with heaters; this board has none (the respin's heater could not sit near the
IMUs, so it was removed), so ArduPilot's software correction, \file{INS\_TCAL*}, is the
whole of the temperature story. It is deliberately \emph{not} in \file{defaults.parm}: it is a
learning mode, not a flight setting.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Run a full six-axis accelerometer calibration first --- it sets the
\file{INS\_ACC*\_CALTEMP} and \file{INS\_GYR*\_CALTEMP} the fit is referenced to.
\item Cool the board below its minimum operating temperature: a domestic freezer, in a
sealed bag against condensation.
\item Set \file{INS\_TCAL1\_ENABLE 2} (\textbf{2 = start learning}, not 1) and
\file{INS\_TCAL1\_TMAX} to the target; the same for \file{INS\_TCAL2\_*} --- there are two IMUs.
\item Power it up in a warm room and \textbf{do not let it move until the run completes.}
Movement invalidates the fit silently.
\item Let it rise through at least 25\degC{} of range, freezer to desk, allowing ten minutes
per temperature point. Then verify the fit with the offline plotting tool: the corrected
traces should sit near zero across the whole range.
\end{enumerate}

Do this with the \textbf{stack assembled}, not the board in free air, because the assembly
\emph{is} the thermal environment.

\section{Part 13 --- T3d: the four unlabelled pads}
\label{sec:T3d}

Of the \textbf{10} pads and test points on the board, \textbf{6} print their function
(\file{L5V}, \file{DIN}, \file{BZ} \dots) at 1.0\,mm. A label is printed only where it is
legal silkscreen \emph{and} sits clearly nearer its own pad than any other part ---
\file{tools/check\_silk\_owner.py} gates that --- because a label that reads as its

\file{TP3} (servo 5)

any other list you read as stale.

\section{Part 14 --- T4/T5: tethered, then flight, strictly in this order}

\subsection{Foam, shroud and coating, after the bench and before the first flight}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Baro foam} (\file{design.FOAM}): a 3\,mm open-cell block over \file{U4}
(MS5611) on the board's underside. It keeps light, prop wash and the ESC's warm air off the
port without sealing it. Glue only its edge.
\item \textbf{IMU shroud}: a 1.5\,mm adhesive closed-cell pad on the ESC's top face, under
\file{U2}, \file{U3} and \file{U4}. \file{check\_mechanical.py} proves both leave over 1\,mm
of air in the ESC gap; neither may touch the board.
\item \textbf{Conformal coating}, only after T3 passes: acrylic with a UV tracer, inspected
under a UV torch. Mask first: every connector mouth (\file{J1}--\file{J23}), the microSD
socket \file{J8}, the MMCX \file{J12}, \file{SW1}/\file{SW2}, the Tag-Connect pads \file{J19},
every test and solder pad (\file{docs/img/padmap.svg}), the four stack holes, and \file{U4}'s
baro port --- coat that and the barometer reads the coating. Coat both sides, let it cure,
then refit the foam.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Staking the plugs}

Before the first tethered hover, check how each plug is held, because a 7-inch quad's
vibration walks a friction plug out over a few flights. On this board nothing needs a bead
of silicone:

\begin{itemize}
\item every JST-GH port --- \file{J2}, \file{J3}, \file{J5}, \file{J6}, \file{J9},
\file{J11}, \file{J14}, \file{J21}, \file{J22} --- latches: check each one clicks, and pull
gently on the lead to prove it;
\item the MMCX on \file{J12} snaps on with a detent; tie the coax down 10--15 mm from it;
\item the servo plugs on \file{J17}/\file{J23} are a friction fit, held by the printed clip
over both --- fit the clip before flight;
\item the LED strip and buzzer are soldered.
\end{itemize}

\file{J1} (USB) and \file{J19} (SWD) are bench ports: unplug them before flight.
\file{tools/check\_connectors.py} fails if a connector is added without saying how it stays
in, or if one declared staked is missing from this list.

\subsection{Order of flights}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Run the SITL scenarios first:} \cmd{bash sitl/run\_scenarios.sh}. It found
three prearm faults no build check saw.
\item \textbf{Battery via the smoke stopper and the EC5-to-XT60 adapter} the first time.
\item \textbf{Tethered hover in Stabilize.} Land. Pull the log.
\item Tune \file{INS\_HNTCH\_FREQ} and \file{INS\_HNTCH\_BW} from the log's FFT.
\item \textbf{GPS Loiter.} This is a working aircraft.
\item \textbf{Props on last}, after motor direction is confirmed.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Everything past GPS Loiter is research.} GNSS-denied position hold is
uncharacterised until T3b is measured.

\subsection{15.4 The three worst, ranked}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \flag{The RF sigma (T3b).} It is the only item that can invalidate the project's
premise rather than a build step, and no amount of desk work substitutes for it. Everything
downstream of it --- position-hold claims, the SITL scenarios' hold/runaway results --- is
explicitly not readable until it is measured.
\item \flag{\file{U8}'s junction temperature.} 87\degC{} at the worst corner against a
125\degC{} recommended maximum --- 38\degC{} of margin, after the 2026-09-20 swap to the
LMR33630ARNXR. It is now a number to confirm rather than one to rescue, and the payload BEC
is what stops a later addition eating the margin.

both are silenced by owning the M3 assortment and a multimeter.

\subsection{15.5 Design-level inefficiencies, for a future revision}

These do not affect this order. They are recorded because a respin is the only place they
can be fixed, and one of them (\file{U8}) may force one anyway.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{USB runs the logic but not the payload rail}, and back-feeds about 4\,V onto
\file{VBAT} and the ESC's battery pin through \file{U8} and \file{Q4}.
\item \textbf{Four pads arrive unlabelled} (Section~\ref{sec:T3d}) because a 1.0\,mm label,
placed where it reads as its own pad's, has nowhere to go among them.
\item \textbf{The payload rail cannot carry every payload connector at once}: 1.43 A is
inside \file{L5}'s 2.1 A, but puts \file{U20} at about 132\,\degC{} against 125\,\degC{}
(\file{check\_modules.py}). Each declared mission fits.
\item \textbf{The Economic variant saves one part and not the feeder fees}, which makes it a
worse deal than it reads as (Section 15.1).
\item \textbf{In4 rail fragmentation} is an accepted warning on this board.
\item \textbf{The 1620 MHz SAW filter is not stocked at LCSC}, so the RF front end needs a
second supplier and a separate board.
\end{itemize}
